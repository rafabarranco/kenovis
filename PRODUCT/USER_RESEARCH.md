User Research Framework

Version: 1.0
---
Purpose

This document defines how the company understands users, validates problems and makes product decisions based on evidence.

The objective is to avoid building features based on assumptions.
---
Research Philosophy

The company follows this principle:

Problems first.
Solutions second.

We do not ask:

"Would you use this feature?"

We ask:

"How do you currently solve this problem?"
---
Initial Market

Primary Market

Music bands.
---

- Easy access to users.
- Strong community networks.
- Clear operational problems.

Target User Profiles
---
Persona 1: Organization Manager

Description

The person responsible for coordinating the organization.

Examples:

- Band director.
- President.
- Coordinator.
- Administrator.
---
Main Responsibilities

- Organizing activities.
- Managing members.
- Communicating information.
- Tracking attendance.
- Managing money.
---
Current Tools

Typical workflow:

- WhatsApp.
- Excel.
- Google Sheets.
- Paper notes.
- Bank transfers.
---
Main Pain Points

Potential problems:

- Losing information in conversations.
- Not knowing who attends.
- Manually calculating payments.
- Repeating the same communication.
- Lack of historical information.
---
Desired Outcome

"I want everything organized in one place."
---
Persona 2: Member

Description

A person participating in the organization.

Examples:

- Musician.
- Student.
- Participant.
---
Main Needs

- Know upcoming events.
- Confirm attendance.
- Receive relevant information.
- Access personal history.
---
Pain Points

- Too many messages.
- Missing important information.

- Lack of visibility.

Desired Outcome

"I want to know what I have to do and when."

Core User Problems
---
Problem 1: Member Management

Current Situation

Organizations maintain member information manually.

Typical solutions:

- Spreadsheets.
- Contact lists.
- Messaging groups.
---
Problem Hypothesis

Organizations need a centralized member database.
---
Validation Questions

Ask:

- How do you currently store member information?
- How often does it change?
- Who manages it?
- What problems happen today?
---
Problem 2: Attendance Management

Current Situation

Attendance is managed through:

- WhatsApp messages.
- Manual lists.
- Memory.
---
Problem Hypothesis

Organizations need a simple attendance workflow.
---
Validation Questions

Ask:

- How do you know who attends an activity?
- How much time do you spend collecting responses?
- What happens when someone does not answer?
- Do attendance records matter historically?
---
Problem 3: Financial Distribution

Current Situation

Payments are manually calculated.

Example:

A concert pays:

3000 €

Participants:

30 people

Someone manually calculates:

100 € per person
---
Problem Hypothesis

Organizations need transparent automated distribution.
---
Validation Questions

Ask:

- How are payments currently divided?
- Who calculates them?
- How long does it take?
- Have mistakes happened?
- Would members value seeing the calculation?

Interview Framework
---
Before Interview

Never:

- Sell the product.
- Explain features.
- Ask leading questions.

The goal is learning.
---
Interview Structure

Introduction

Explain:

"We are researching how organizations manage their daily operations."
---
Current Workflow

Questions:

- How do you currently manage members?
- How do you organize activities?
- How do you know who participates?
- How do you handle payments?
---
Pain Discovery

Questions:

- What is the most annoying part?
- What takes the most time?
- What mistakes happen?
- What would you eliminate?
---
Solution Validation

Only after understanding problems:

Questions:

- Would a tool solving this be valuable?
- How would it need to work?
- Who would decide to use it?
- Would the organization pay for it?
---
Evidence Hierarchy

Not all feedback has equal value.

Priority:

1. Existing behavior

2. Existing workaround

3. Money/time spent solving problem

4. Stated interest

5. Opinions

Actions are stronger than opinions.
---
Product Validation Signals

Strong signals:

- Users actively use the product.
- Users invite others.
- Users complain when something is unavailable.
- Users ask for more capabilities.
- Users pay.

Weak signals:

- "Interesting idea."
- "Looks useful."
- "I would probably use it."
---
Early Customer Development Strategy

First customers should be:

- Accessible.
- Representative.
- Willing to provide feedback.

The objective is not revenue initially.

The objective is learning.
---
Feedback Management

All feedback should be classified.

Categories:

Bug

Something does not work.
---
Usability Problem

Something is difficult.
---
Feature Request

A user wants additional capability.
---
New Problem Discovery

A deeper operational problem appears.
---
Feature Prioritization Based on Research

A feature gains priority when:

- Multiple users report the same problem.
- The problem happens frequently.
- Users already use a workaround.
- The problem creates measurable cost.
- The solution improves retention or revenue.

Research Rules for AI Agents

AI agents must:

- Question assumptions.
- Ask for evidence.
- Avoid inventing user needs.
- Prefer validated problems.
- Distinguish facts from hypotheses.
---
Final Principle

The customer is the source of truth.

The product roadmap follows validated problems.

Not assumptions.

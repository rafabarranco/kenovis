User Research Framework

Version: 1.0
---
Purpose

This document defines how the company understands users, validates problems and makes product decisions based on evidence.

The objective is to avoid building features based on assumptions.
---
Research Philosophy

The company follows this principle:

Problems first.
Solutions second.

We do not ask:

"Would you use this feature?"

We ask:

"How do you currently solve this problem?"

Interview Framework
---
Before Interview

Never:

- Sell the product.
- Explain features.
- Ask leading questions.

The goal is learning.
---
Interview Structure

Introduction

Explain:

"We are researching how [target users] manage [the relevant part of their work]."
---
Current Workflow

Questions:

[Questions about how the user currently does the relevant work, without mentioning the product.]
---
Pain Discovery

Questions:

- What is the most annoying part?
- What takes the most time?
- What mistakes happen?
- What would you eliminate?
---
Solution Validation

Only after understanding problems:

Questions:

- Would a tool solving this be valuable?
- How would it need to work?
- Who would decide to use it?
- Would the organization pay for it?
---
Evidence Hierarchy

Not all feedback has equal value.

Priority:

1. Existing behavior

2. Existing workaround

3. Money/time spent solving problem

4. Stated interest

5. Opinions

Actions are stronger than opinions.
---
Product Validation Signals

Strong signals:

- Users actively use the product.
- Users invite others.
- Users complain when something is unavailable.
- Users ask for more capabilities.
- Users pay.

Weak signals:

- "Interesting idea."
- "Looks useful."
- "I would probably use it."
---
Early Customer Development Strategy

First customers should be:

- Accessible.
- Representative.
- Willing to provide feedback.

The objective is not revenue initially.

The objective is learning.
---
Feedback Management

All feedback should be classified.

Categories:

Bug

Something does not work.
---
Usability Problem

Something is difficult.
---
Feature Request

A user wants additional capability.
---
New Problem Discovery

A deeper operational problem appears.
---
Feature Prioritization Based on Research

A feature gains priority when:

- Multiple users report the same problem.
- The problem happens frequently.
- Users already use a workaround.
- The problem creates measurable cost.
- The solution improves retention or revenue.

The company operates this cycle:

Observe Users

↓

Identify Problems

Build Small Solutions

Measure Usage

Learn

Improve

---
Research Rules for AI Agents

AI agents must:

- Question assumptions.
- Ask for evidence.
- Avoid inventing user needs.
- Prefer validated problems.
- Distinguish facts from hypotheses.
---
Final Principle

The customer is the source of truth.

The product roadmap follows validated problems.

Not assumptions.
